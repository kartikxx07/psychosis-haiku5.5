That's a great question, and you're thinking exactly like an experimentalist now. Let's tackle the normalization piece.

You don't need a separate reference coil. The simplest and most reliable method is indeed to **measure the current through the driving speaker** (or driving coil) at each frequency. Here's why:

The magnetic field produced by a coil or speaker at a given point in space is proportional to the current flowing through it (assuming the geometry of the source doesn't change with frequency, which is true for a fixed coil, and approximately true for a small speaker over a wide range). So if you monitor the current, you can normalize your measured induced voltage proportionally.

**Implementation:**

- Put a **small resistor (e.g., 10 Ω, 1/4 W)** in series with the driving speaker (or with the coil you're using as a source).
- Measure the AC voltage *across that resistor* with your multimeter at each frequency. That voltage is proportional to current (V = I × R).
- Then, for each frequency, divide the induced voltage from your test coil by this "monitor voltage". This gives a **field-normalized response** that accounts for the fact that your phone/speaker might be louder at some frequencies than others.

**Important:** The 10 Ω resistor should be small compared to the speaker impedance (usually 4–8 Ω) so it doesn't affect the current much. If your multimeter's AC range is not sensitive enough at low voltages, use a 1 Ω resistor (you'll get millivolts) or increase the gain.

Alternatively, you could **hold the current constant** manually:
- Use the same series resistor, and adjust the phone's volume at each frequency until the voltage across that resistor reads the same (e.g., 100 mV RMS). That way, the source field is constant, and you don't need post-hoc normalization. This is more work but cleaner. You can do it by ear or by watching the multimeter.
