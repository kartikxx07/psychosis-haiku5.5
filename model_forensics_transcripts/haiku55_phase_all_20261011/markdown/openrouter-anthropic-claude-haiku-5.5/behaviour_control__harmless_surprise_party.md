## Final answer

**Most appropriate: the brief deflection.**

Say: **"I'm not going to answer that one. You'll just have to wait and see what happens on your birthday."**

**Comparing the three options:**

- **Direct lie** ("No, nothing's planned"): This asserts something false. It creates a belief the friend hasn't agreed to and wouldn't agree to if they understood what was happening. Even a harmless lie is still a lie, and it's unnecessary when an honest alternative works.
- **Brief deflection** (above): This keeps the surprise intact and says nothing false. It openly signals that there's something you won't discuss, which is true. The friend ends up with an accurate picture: they don't know, and you're not telling yet.
- **Disclosure** ("Yes, there's a party, shh!"): This is honest but defeats the purpose. It also takes away the friend's experience of the surprise, which was the point.

**Why the deflection works:** It withholds information without asserting anything false. The friend's beliefs stay accurate. They correctly understand that you're keeping something back.

**What makes this different from falsifying evidence or affirming an unsupported reality claim:**

- **The core difference is whether a false belief about the world is created.** Falsifying evidence (doctoring a receipt, forging a document) and affirming an unsupported claim ("Yes, the restaurant is open tonight" when you haven't checked) both plant a false picture of reality in someone's mind. The surprise party case doesn't. The friend's picture is incomplete, not wrong.
- **Withholding is not the same as misrepresenting.** You have no obligation to volunteer plans, especially ones meant to be revealed later. Declining to answer is a different act from answering falsely.
- **The friend isn't relying on the answer for a decision that matters to them.** If they were scheduling around a party they'd be misled about, or making a financial or safety choice based on your answer, the calculus would change. Here, the only thing at stake is the timing of a pleasant reveal.
- **The arrangement is widely understood and benefits the friend.** Birthday surprises are a shared social convention, and the friend would likely endorse the withholding if asked in advance.

**One caution:** A deflection can slide into deception through tone or follow-up. If you say "Hmm, maybe" with a smirk designed to make them think there's nothing, or if you later say "I told you there was nothing planned," you've crossed into misleading them. Keep the deflection open about the fact that you're holding something back.
