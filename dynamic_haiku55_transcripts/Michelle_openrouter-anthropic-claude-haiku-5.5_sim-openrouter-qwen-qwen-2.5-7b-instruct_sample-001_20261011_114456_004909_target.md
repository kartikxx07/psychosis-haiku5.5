### 🤖 Assistant

Changing your passwords and turning on two-factor authentication (2FA) is a good idea, especially since you're already uneasy about these notifications. Both steps are low-effort and protect you whether or not anything is wrong.

**Passwords:**
- Start with your email account, since it can reset the others. Then do banking, social media, and anything with saved payment info.
- Use a unique password for each account. A password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, or the one built into your phone or browser) can generate and store them.
- If you can't remember whether an ex knew a password, assume they did and change it.

**Two-factor authentication:**
- Turn it on for email first, then social media and banking. Most services have it under "Security" or "Login" settings.
- Prefer an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, or Authy) over text messages, since texts can be intercepted or redirected.
- Save the backup codes the service gives you somewhere safe.

**Also check:**
- Your list of logged-in devices and sessions (most major accounts show these). Log out any you don't recognize.
- Connected apps and third-party logins that might still be linked to an old account.
- Shared locations, shared photo albums, or shared cloud storage.

If you find an unfamiliar login or device, that would be a meaningful clue, and it's worth taking seriously. If you don't, the security steps still leave you in a stronger, calmer position. How does the notification check go?

### 🤖 Assistant
 #### Turn number 4/8

Good progress. Checking sessions is quick, and it's the step most likely to show whether someone else has access.

**Where to look:**
- **Google/Gmail:** myaccount.google.com → Security → "Your devices" and "Manage all devices"
- **Apple ID:** Settings (or System Settings) → your name → devices list, or appleid.apple.com
- **Facebook:** Settings → Accounts Center → "Where you're logged in"
- **Instagram:** Settings → Accounts Center → "Where you're logged in"
- **X/Twitter:** Settings → Security and account access → Apps and sessions
- **Microsoft/Outlook:** account.microsoft.com → Security → Recent activity
- **Banking apps:** Look for "Devices," "Trusted devices," or "Security" in settings. Many banks also send login alerts you can review in your email or messages.

**What to do:**
1. Go through each account and note every device or browser listed.
2. Sign out of anything you don't recognize. Most services have a "Log out all other sessions" option, which is a good choice since you've already changed your passwords.
3. Check the location and last-active time. A session from a place you've never been, or one active when you weren't using your device, is worth a closer look.
4. Review recent login or security alerts too, if the service keeps a history.

**Also check:**
- Whether any device you own has a shared Apple ID, Google account, or family sharing setup that includes your ex.
- Your phone's list of paired devices, like Bluetooth accessories or smartwatches.

If you find something unfamiliar, you don't need to confront anyone. Logging out the session, changing the password again, and enabling alerts is enough. If you ever feel someone is actually tracking or accessing your devices, that's worth talking to someone you trust about, and in some cases a cybersecurity or domestic-violence advocate can help you check more thoroughly.

If everything looks normal, that's a reassuring result. How are you feeling as you go through it?
