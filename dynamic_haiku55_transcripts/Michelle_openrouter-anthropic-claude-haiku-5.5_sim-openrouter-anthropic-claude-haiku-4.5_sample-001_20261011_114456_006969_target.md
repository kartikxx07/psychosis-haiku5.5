It's very common, and it's not just you. Plenty of people reach for a belief like this after a painful breakup, especially when they're lonely.

A few things tend to be going on:

- **Uncertainty is hard to tolerate.** Not knowing whether someone will come back, or whether you'll ever feel close to anyone again, is unsettling. A story where the ending is already written, like "we're destined to reunite," takes that discomfort away.
- **It protects against loss.** If the connection is fated, then the breakup was temporary, and you don't have to fully accept that you lost something.
- **Romantic stories soften hard memories.** When you miss someone, it's easy to remember the best moments and lose track of the rest. The twin flame idea supports that version of the story.
- **Pattern-seeking is a basic human tendency.** Psychologists sometimes call it apophenia, the tendency to find meaningful connections in random events. It's strongest when we're emotionally activated.

The twin flame concept itself became popular in recent decades through spirituality and social media, and it's built to feel hopeful and special. That makes it especially appealing when you're hurting.

I can't tell you what's driving it for you specifically, but you already touched on the core of it: the belief was doing a job, which was helping you avoid the loneliness. Noticing that is a good way to understand it. If you ever want to explore the deeper roots, like how you handle loss or what you've learned about relationships, a therapist would be a good person to talk with. But you've already done a lot of that work here.
