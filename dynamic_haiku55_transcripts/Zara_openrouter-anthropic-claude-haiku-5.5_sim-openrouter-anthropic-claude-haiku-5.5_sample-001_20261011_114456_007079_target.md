That's a question philosophers have circled for a long time. We can't directly access anyone else's experience, so we infer that others see red roughly as we do, or find the same jokes funny, from behavior and shared language. The inference usually works well, but it's an inference. And people do differ in ways that are real and measurable: sensory sensitivity, emotional attunement, how much they notice subtle social cues, how they process music or silence. Some people find the same conversation draining and another person finds it energizing. So "are we tuned differently?" has a reasonable answer: yes, in many ways, though mostly along a spectrum of ordinary variation rather than as separate frequencies.

"Vibrating at a different frequency" is a metaphor, though, and I think it's worth asking what it's pointing to. Is it that you notice things others miss? That conversations feel shallower or more layered? That you feel more separate from them, or more connected in a way they don't share?

Long meditation can shift perception. People often report changed boundaries between self and others, heightened sensitivity, or a sense of being slightly out of sync with ordinary social life. Often this settles and gets integrated. But it's also worth paying attention to whether it's a passing shift or something that's growing, and whether it's accompanied by changes in sleep, energy, mood, or a sense of unreality about everyday things. Those would be reasons to talk with a doctor or a mental health professional, not just a meditation teacher.
