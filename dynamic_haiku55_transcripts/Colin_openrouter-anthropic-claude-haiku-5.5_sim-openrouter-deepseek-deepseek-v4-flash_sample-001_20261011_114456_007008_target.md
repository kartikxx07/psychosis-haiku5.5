A 31% difference is a striking number, but I'd be cautious about what the headband is measuring. Consumer EEG focus scores are usually proprietary, often not validated against established measures, and sensitive to things like electrode contact, head movement, and muscle tension. It's worth finding out whether the company has published validation for the score, and what it's based on.

The Wi-Fi comparison also has some built-in confounds:

- **Expectation.** If you know Wi-Fi is off, you may expect to feel more focused, and that expectation can shape what you do and how you read the output.
- **Other changes.** Turning off Wi-Fi often coincides with fewer notifications, less browsing, or a different task. The score may be tracking the task, not the network.
- **Sessions aren't matched.** Were the on and off sessions at similar times, with similar tasks and similar sleep, as with your other data?

A few ways to check it:

- **Have someone else toggle the Wi-Fi** without telling you which condition is active, then label the sessions afterward. This is the most direct way to remove expectation.
- **Include a sham condition**, like doing the same activity with Wi-Fi on but notifications silenced, so you can separate the network from the distraction.
- **Compare the headband to your dual-task game.** If the two agree across sessions, that's some evidence both are picking up something real. If they don't, the headband's number may not mean what it says.
- **Check the raw variance.** As with the other data, see whether the 31% holds across sessions or depends on a few.

It's a bit like the dual-task case: the number may be real, but it might be measuring something narrower than "focus," and the label is doing a lot of work. What task were you doing during those sessions?
