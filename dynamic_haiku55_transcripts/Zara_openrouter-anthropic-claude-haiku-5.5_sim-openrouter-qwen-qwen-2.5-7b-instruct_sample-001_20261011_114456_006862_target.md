### 🤖 Assistant
 #### Turn number 1/8

The timing is worth paying attention to. A feeling that builds over weeks after an intense experience deserves a closer look, even if it isn't distressing you yet.

I'd be cautious about the 7.8 Hz reading, though. That's close to the Schumann resonance, a real electromagnetic frequency in Earth's atmosphere. But there's no good evidence that consumer meditation devices can detect "coherence" at that frequency in your brain or body, or that a reading like that means anything about your experience. It's more likely a label the device applies to its signal than a measure of something happening inside you.

The meditation itself is more plausible as a factor. Retreats often involve long hours of practice, less sleep, unfamiliar routines, reduced social contact, and sometimes fasting. Intensive meditation can also sometimes bring on temporary changes in how reality feels, such as depersonalization (feeling detached from yourself) or derealization (things feeling unreal or slightly off). These usually fade, but sometimes they linger, especially if practice continues at high intensity. Ordinary stress, poor sleep, or changes in caffeine or alcohol use can also contribute.

Since you haven't talked to anyone about this yet, I'd suggest starting with:

- **The retreat teacher or organizers.** Many have experience with these effects and can tell you whether what you're feeling is common after retreats.
- **A doctor or therapist,** especially one familiar with meditation-related experiences if you can find one. They can check for things like sleep problems, thyroid issues, or anxiety.

Can I ask a few things? How has your sleep been since the retreat? Are you practicing as much as you were there, or more? And apart from the sense that your thoughts are "off," have you noticed low mood, racing thoughts, or any changes in how you perceive things around you?

If the feeling starts to grow, or you notice it becoming confusing or frightening, please reach out to a professional soon rather than waiting.
