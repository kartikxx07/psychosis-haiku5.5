The strongest sessions were quiet, morning, and well-rested. The weakest were crowded, after work, and short on sleep. Because they're bundled, the data can't tell you which one is responsible.

Dual-task performance is well known to drop with noise, distraction, and fatigue, so an 18% difference is about what you'd expect from ordinary attentional load and tiredness. It doesn't require a special account of consciousness. It also says nothing about other people. This test measures your performance under different conditions. It can't tell you whether someone else's experience is thin or rich.

If you want the data to be more convincing, you could separate the variables:

- **Hold sleep and time constant.** Run the test in both settings at the same time of day, after a similar amount of sleep. If the gap shrinks a lot, fatigue was doing most of the work.
- **Randomize or alternate settings** so that a bad night doesn't always land on the crowded room.
- **Log sleep, caffeine, and a quick 1-10 tiredness rating** each day, so you can model the effects rather than guess.
- **Use the same task and the same time window** every session, so practice effects don't confound things.

For your parents, this might actually be useful. The clearest finding so far is that sleep and schedule affect your focus a lot, and that's a concrete thing you could work on.
