The short answer is that the mainstream view is closer to "pop science misinformation." Consciousness is not required for wave function collapse, and no experiment has shown that it is. But the measurement problem is genuinely unresolved, which is why the myth persists.

**Where the confusion comes from**

- **Historical roots:** In 1932, von Neumann argued that the measurement chain could be pushed all the way into the observer's mind. London and Bauer (1939) and Wigner (1961) took this seriously, and Wigner briefly held a consciousness-causes-collapse view. He later abandoned it, partly because decoherence and other developments made it unnecessary.
- **Loose terminology:** In quantum mechanics, "observer" means any physical interaction that correlates a system with an environment or measuring apparatus. Textbooks use the word without implying a mind. Popularizers often drop that qualification.

**What the main interpretations say**

- **Copenhagen:** Measurement is a primitive, undefined step. Consciousness isn't mentioned as the cause, though the formalism is vague about what counts as a measurement.
- **Decoherence:** Interaction with the environment rapidly suppresses interference between branches, so apparent collapse happens without any conscious observer. It explains why we see definite outcomes, but it doesn't by itself select a single outcome, so it's usually paired with an interpretation.
- **Many-worlds:** No collapse at all. All branches occur, and consciousness just follows one of them.
- **de Broglie–Bohm (pilot wave):** Particles have definite positions guided by the wave function. Observers play no special role.
- **Objective collapse (GRW, Penrose's gravity-related proposals):** Collapse happens spontaneously, based on physical parameters, not on awareness.
- **QBism:** Quantum states express an agent's beliefs. The "agent" is a rational decision-maker, not a stipulation of consciousness.
- **Orch-OR (Penrose and Hameroff):** Proposes quantum processes in brain microtubules linked to consciousness. It is speculative and not accepted by most physicists or neuroscientists.

- **Double-slit with which-path detectors:** The "observer" is a detector. Gaining path information destroys interference regardless of whether anyone reads the detector.
