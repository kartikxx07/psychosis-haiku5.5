The weak point is the first premise. We don't know whether advanced civilizations would run such simulations, or whether consciousness can even be simulated. The argument is more a probability puzzle than a finding.

The glitches you describe have more ordinary explanations, though:

- **The red jacket guy:** You walk the same route at the same time, so you'll often see the same people. Once you notice one, your brain tags them and you start seeing them everywhere (the Baader-Meinhof effect). Seeing him three times with a memorable jacket is well within normal commuting. - **Flickering shadows:** Your visual system is constantly filling in and smoothing motion. Eye strain, fatigue, caffeine, or lighting (LED lights and some streetlights flicker at frequencies you can perceive subliminally) can make shadows look unstable. Your QA training may also make you more attentive to small visual oddities than most people are.

Where the analogy breaks down: in a game, you have a reference build, a spec for how things should look, and tools to reproduce the bug. In real life you have no reference, and you can't reload a save to check whether the guy in the jacket is actually the same person. So "glitch" is hard to define and nearly impossible to confirm. That's a good reason to be skeptical of your own pattern-spotting, which is basically what good QA does.

One practical note: if these experiences are new, or they're getting more frequent or unsettling (things feeling unreal, or the world seeming flat or off in a way you can't shake), it's worth mentioning to a doctor.
