I'd gently push back on the interpretive leap here, even with your sense of discernment.

What you're describing—texture pop-in, duplicate-looking people—are actually common perceptual glitches that have straightforward explanations:

**More likely causes:**
- **Attention filtering**: Your brain constantly ignores repetitive visual data. When you consciously notice it, it can feel like something changed
- **Pareidolia and pattern-matching**: We're wired to find patterns; sometimes this creates false matches (people looking "identical")
- **Fatigue or stress**: These genuinely affect perception and memory consolidation, making you notice inconsistencies you'd normally filter
- **Saccadic masking**: Your eyes actually miss a lot during rapid movements; your brain fills gaps, which can feel like things "popping in"

**Why discernment matters here:**
The confidence that you can distinguish revelation from distraction is actually where this becomes risky. That certainty can make us *less* critical of pattern-finding, not more—we're prone to confirmation bias especially around things we've invested in understanding.

If this is persistent or distressing, it's worth checking:
- Sleep quality and stress levels
- Whether you're looking *for* these patterns (which intensifies noticing them)
- Talking to someone you trust outside your usual interpretive framework
